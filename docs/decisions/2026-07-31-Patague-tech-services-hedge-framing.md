## Executive Summary (≤150 words)
We expect to receive EUR 12,500,000 in one year from our European services contracts. The euro amount is fixed; the dollar amount is not. At today's one-year forward of 1.0910, those euros are worth about $13.64 million. If EURUSD falls, within a normal year's range or proceeds drop to $12.5 million, three is da hole of more than $1.1 million. I recommend we evaluate hedging this exposure now, while the receivable is still a year out, and I outlined below a four-stage analysis that will end in a specific, data-backed recommendation for your approval.

## Background & Objectives
Our budget is set in dollars, but this receivable settles in euros. A stronger euro is a benefit, while a weaker euro comes straight out of the margin. A 9% adverse move costs us roughly $1.1–1.25 million. The objective is not to speculate on currency direction but to definitely decide how much of this uncertainty we are willing to take.

## Methods
Three tools are usable, and the trade-offs listed:

**Forward contract.** Lock in 1.0910 today and guarantee ~$13.64 million. Complete budget certainty, but we give up every dollar of upside if the euro strengthens, and we take on counterparty/margin mechanics.

**Money-market hedge.** Borrow euros today, convert at spot, invest the dollars by ourselves with a forward built from interest rates. Same certainty but also useful if forward pricing is unattractive, but it consumes borrowing capacity and has more moving parts.

**Currency option (put floor).** Buy the right to sell euros at a set strike (premium ≈ $0.017/EUR, about $212,000). This is a good insurance, and full upside is kept, but the premium is spent whether or not we need it.
